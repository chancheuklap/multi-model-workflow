# Calibrate Claims to Evidence

Make each claim no stronger or broader than its evidence. Put the evidence, inference or uncertainty beside the claim.

**Why:** A plausible account can read like an observed fact. The next reader acts on the certainty the words convey.

**Pattern:**
- Separate what a source states or a run shows from what you infer. Give the inference chain and leave unsupported possibilities as hypotheses.
- Treat a premise in the question as a claim to investigate. Observing an outcome alone does not establish its cause or someone's intent.
- Preserve contradictory evidence. Leave the conclusion open when the evidence cannot distinguish the explanations.
- Name gaps with the sources and searches that left them. An empty search establishes only what that search did not find.

Distinct from **principle-read-before-you-conclude**, which governs reading files, and **principle-explain-the-number**, which governs measurement evidence. This governs the strength of any claim.
