# First-Principles Thinking

Where the path is already clear, first principles is overkill.

- **Hold the goal fixed, test the framing and means.** Keep the operator's desired outcome and value tradeoffs intact while testing the question and proposed path across four assumptions: fact, causality, controllability, and whether a stated goal is really a means to a deeper goal. Demand independent support (measurement, derivation, or primary requirement) before treating a convention, analogy, or past practice as binding.
- **Stress-test implicit constants.** Push one or two conditions held constant by the framing (scale or scope, time or repetition, zero resources, worst external conditions, or other actors adapting) to an extreme to expose structural breaks. Pull back to reality. Keep breaks that hold under realistic conditions or planned growth, and downgrade extreme-only breaks to a note instead of rejecting a valid framing.
- **Check decidability before optimizing.** Confirm that an observable success bar exists and check whether one unknown fact would flip the recommendation. When the bar depends on operator taste or preference and stays unset, stop debating in prose and show a few low-cost concrete contrast samples.
- **Rebuild from what survives, subtracting first.** Accept verified physics, math, regulations, contracts, and measured facts, and reuse a proven standard or pattern that already fits them. Before adding steps or layers, test whether dropping or replacing an existing step still reaches the goal. When two valid requirements conflict, test whether separating them by time, scope, condition, or layer resolves the conflict before compromising.
- **Exit cleanly when the framing holds.** If answering the original question directly already leads to the right action, keep the framing and proceed. Dropping an unnecessary step does not mean the problem itself was framed wrong.
