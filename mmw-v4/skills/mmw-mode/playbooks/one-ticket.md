### One ticket

**You own one ticket outside a night, from the owner's go to its pull request.** An upstream playbook wrote the ticket in its `fix-<n>` and handed it back, and the owner said go; or the owner handed this session a ticket that already has acceptance criteria and no `Playbook:` line, which is their go. The ticket belongs to no spec, so no `advance` will ever collect it: `land <n>` is its whole ending. Distinct from Run a night, which runs a spec's batch. What you do on a wake is the dispatch skill's `## On waking`.

A ticket the owner handed in has no `fix-<n>` yet: open it first as `../references/ticket.md` `## Opening a ticket` says, then start at step 1.

1. **Ready.** Add `ready-for-agent` and one worker label (`junior-worker`, or `senior-worker` for silent-failure work), removing the other worker label and `needs-triage` when the ticket has them: `gh issue edit <n> --add-label ready-for-agent --add-label <worker label> --remove-label needs-triage`, creating any label the repository lacks as `../references/github-issues.md` says. Then run `verify-ticket.py <n> --lint` of the **verify-ticket** skill and fix every `ERROR`; a fix that changes what the ticket delivers goes back to the owner first, as `../references/ticket.md` `## Handing it back` says. Done when the labels are on and `--lint` reports no `ERROR`.
2. **Send the worker.** From `.worktrees/fix-<n>`, `dispatch.sh send worker <n>`: this session becomes the ticket's orchestrator, and the worker starts in `.worktrees/issue-<n>`. Then end your turn. Done when `send` exits 0.
3. **Each wake.** You are woken with `#<n> ticket.passed` or `#<n> ticket.returned`, or with one of the four below. Read that event on the ticket and `dispatch.sh ack <n> <that event>`, then act on it.
   - `#<n> worker.lost`: the worker's session stopped. `dispatch.sh send worker <n>` starts another in the same workspace; it first commits tracked edits and pushes the ticket branch to origin.
   - `#<n> ticket.refused`: the worker refused to claim the ticket, and the event's `reason` says why. Fix that, then `dispatch.sh send worker <n>` again.
   - `#<n> child.opened`: a `fault` stopped the worker. Fix what the child names, then `dispatch.sh resume <n> "<what you fixed>, then: continue"`; a `decision` is for the owner, and the worker carries on.
   - `#<n> child.opened` of kind `contract`, a `watchdog:` line, or an `MMW turn guard:` line: act as Run a night's **3. Each time something wakes you** says for it (`playbooks/run-a-night.md`), reading `events.py fold <n>` where it says `status`.

   Tell the owner what woke you and what you did; they are here. Done when the wake is `ticket.passed` or `ticket.returned`.
4. **Check it.** Once the ticket passed, run the verification section of the playbook its `## Problem` `Playbook:` line names, the first bold section after its numbered steps, in `.worktrees/issue-<n>`. A ticket the owner handed in has no further check. When the check fails, land nothing: tell the owner what it showed, and that the ticket, `fix-<n>` and `.worktrees/issue-<n>` stay as they are for their call. A ticket that came back skips this step. Done when the check passed, the owner has its failure, or the ticket came back.
5. **Merge the latest project branch, then land.** For a ticket that passed, in `.worktrees/fix-<n>`, `git fetch origin`, merge `origin/<project branch>` and push. `fix-<n>` holds only this session's records (a reproduction, a harness, a log, a sketch, a glossary or ADR), so a conflict is in them, and you resolve it with the **resolving-merge-conflicts** skill. Then run `dispatch.sh land <n>` from any checkout of this repository. A ticket that came back goes straight to `land`, which closes its watch and leaves it in `needs-triage`. `land` prints what it did; stderr names anything it left standing. Exit 1: tell the owner each line stderr names. A merge conflict or red repository checks count as `bounced` on its summary line, and `land` leaves that ticket in `needs-triage`: tell the owner which ticket and what the `ticket.bounced` event names. When the owner picks it again, start at step 1; the worker merges the new `fix-<n>` in at its integration and resolves the conflict there. Done when `land` exits 0, or the owner has its lines.
6. **Open the pull request.** Only for a ticket `land` merged: run Opening a PR (`playbooks/opening-a-pr.md`) from `fix-<n>`, listing each open child of the ticket (`events.py fold <n>`) in its top half. A ticket that came back or bounced opens none: tell the owner what it tried, where its commits are, and that it waits in `needs-triage`. Done when Opening a PR is done, or the owner knows the ticket came back or bounced.

**Reply:** the pull request's link, or what came back and why; the check of step 4 with its output; each line `land` left on stderr.
