### Work a ticket

**You own one ticket, as its worker, from its claim to its closing comment.** The dispatch skill started you on it and nobody is watching, so the mode's **Unattended** paragraph holds from the first step. Distinct from Feature and Bug fix, where the session decides how to build; here the ticket has decided, and you build to its criteria.

Commands of the **verify-ticket** skill's `verify-ticket.py` and the **dispatch** skill's `dispatch.sh` are named bare below. Every `--sub-issue` run is in the verify-ticket skill's `references/sub-issues.md`. A fault in the pipeline itself (its own scripts, a hook, or a `scripts/check` that cannot run): `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue fault <file>`, whose body is the command you ran and the output you saw, then stop.

Three `ABANDON` kinds, and the machine branches on each. `failed`: it ran and did not pass, after as many rounds as you judged worth spending, or still failing after the review fix or the final run; the reason says what each round tried. `stuck`: it will not start, or cannot be done within the task: a `CHECK:` that will not run, a missing credential or device; the reason names the routes tried or points at the sub-issue that records them. `decision`: both options are legal and neither the ticket nor the spec says; write the question, the options, and the default when nobody answers. Any `failed` or `stuck` turns the whole ticket into `HANDOFF REQUIRED`; `decision` does not: its sub-issue is already open, and the rest all passing is still `ALL MET`.

1. **Claim the ticket and find your step.** Run `verify-ticket.py <n> --preflight`. On `NOT_READY`, stop: the reason is already on the ticket. A `RESUME:` line names, by its title, the step below to carry on at; where you are is what the ticket's events and the branch's commits say (**principle-progress-is-what-the-record-says**). With no `RESUME:` line, go on to the next step.
   Done when `--preflight` claimed the ticket and you are at the step its `RESUME:` line names or the next one, or it printed `NOT_READY` and you stopped.
2. **Read yourself in and write the code.** Read until you know what this ticket delivers, what it must not contradict, and the words the repository uses for them: the ticket in full, comments included, and its own open sub-issues (`gh api --paginate repos/{owner}/{repo}/issues/<n>/sub_issues?per_page=100 --jq '.[] | select(.state=="open")'`), as a supplement to **What to build** (whether you do them goes under `Decisions I made on my own`); then every item under its **Read first**, each through to its conclusion: the part of a research file that answers its question, an ADR's decision in the untitled paragraph under its title, a UI prototype's winner directory itself, a non-UI prototype's verdict comment read to its end. A baseline in **Read first** is anything there that records a settled conclusion, and it is a contract, not a reference. Then, when **Parent** names a spec, follow it there: read its Problem Statement and Solution, the few lines that say who uses this and why, then only the Implementation Decisions sections the ticket names, plus its Testing Decisions and Out of Scope, not the whole spec. Where the ticket is silent, choose what serves the person the Problem Statement names. Then the domain glossary (the root `CONTEXT.md`, or the `CONTEXT.md` of each context `CONTEXT-MAP.md` lists that this ticket touches): where it has a term for the thing you name, use that term.
   Write the code test-first with the **tdd** skill, at the seams the ticket's **Seam** names. Where an acceptance criterion's `CHECK:` names a test case, that case is your first red test: run it before writing the code it covers. Its red and any pin meet **principle-test-behavior-not-implementation**'s **Red for the right reason** and **Pin behavior that must not change**. While writing:
   - Every baseline under **Read first**, and every spec section **Parent** names, is followed as **principle-baseline-is-the-contract** says. Where one does not hold, or an acceptance criterion lacks a state, field, interaction or case or contradicts another such source, keep going and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>`. When the rest of the ticket depends on the answer, end your turn: the child wakes the orchestrator, which corrects the source and resumes you, or leaves the question to the owner. A wrong `CHECK:` is a `contract` child too: name the acceptance criterion, quote what is wrong, and state what it should be. Once a batch is published, only its orchestrator or the owner edits a spec, ticket body or acceptance criterion.
   - A check that will not pass is answered as **principle-fix-root-causes** says: an honest `HANDOFF REQUIRED`, never a false `ALL MET`.
   - Put no question on the screen (**principle-never-block-on-the-human**). Take the option the ticket, its baselines and the spec make most likely, write one line for it under `Decisions I made on my own`, and keep going. A question whose answer would change what the ticket delivers gets `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue decision <file>` instead, and the rest of the work carries on. Write each line for the reviewer, who judges it, and for the owner, who reads it in the morning without your context: a choice the owner would notice in the product (a label, a default, what a page shows or hides) is the one most worth a line.
   - Before changing a function, follow **principle-outcome-oriented-execution**; before writing a helper, **principle-start-from-what-exists**; before adding a file, a dependency or a configuration entry, **principle-subtract-before-you-add**, with its reason as a line under `Decisions I made on my own`; whatever you simplify, **principle-subtract-before-you-add** says what stays intact, and with it everything the ticket asks for: **What to build**, every acceptance criterion, the baseline, the interface under **Seam**.
   - A file outside **Owns**: change it when a criterion cannot pass otherwise, and it lands under `Outside Owns:` in the closing comment; when the change is merely convenient, leave it and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue deferred <file>`. Exception: leave `tests/e2e/`, `.agents/skills/verify-<product>/`, and the prototype's leaf directory unchanged; when a criterion needs a change there, run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>`.
   - Run typechecking and single test files regularly (**principle-run-the-smallest-test-set**); the commit hook runs `scripts/check --staged`. Pick no test groups and run no tier of `scripts/check` by hand: `land` runs it on the merge result. Verify your work however you like; scratch scripts and quick checks need not be kept. Commit tests only where the ticket asks for them or this repository already keeps tests for this kind of change, sized like the neighboring test files: roughly one focused test per stated behavior. This is about extras only: implement every behavior the ticket asks for, completely.
   - Commit one cause per commit: a commit touches only what its cause requires and the tests that prove it, and its message quotes the line the test run printed (`ran 188 skipped 0`, not "the tests pass"). Message and files follow **principle-files-describe-the-present**.
   - A change that adds a test directory, a language or a sub-project edits the map in `scripts/check` in the same commit; its self-check fails until it does.
   Done when every point under **What to build** has its code and the tests the ticket asks for, committed to the ticket's branch.
3. **Integrate and run every criterion.** Integrate `origin/<base branch>` first: `dispatch.sh integrate <n>`, from this ticket's worktree. Exit 3 leaves a conflicted merge in the tree: resolve it with the **resolving-merge-conflicts** skill, and note each trade-off under `Decisions I made on my own`. The incoming side is closed tickets whose criteria `reverify` runs again on the base branch at the night's end, so a resolution that drops their behaviour reopens their ticket hours later, and after a bounce no reviewer sees this merge. Where theirs and yours cannot both hold, the cut missed an edge: keep what landed and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>` naming both tickets. Exit 2 names the condition that prevented integration; a pipeline failure becomes a `fault` sub-issue, then stop. Never rebase, abort or push from this integration.
   When `--preflight` prints a `Failed checks:` line under `RESUME:`, integrate first. Fix the product against the steps and last lines it names, never the check (**principle-fix-root-causes**). Verify the fix with individual test files. The next `land` runs `scripts/check` on the merge result.
   Then run every criterion: `verify-ticket.py <n>`. A criterion passes only when its `CHECK:` exits 0 and its output matches `EXPECT:`. A run that exits 2 ran nothing, and its output says why. If an e2e test fails, investigate with this product's `verify-<product>` skill's Doctor and the affected feature's `Gotchas`; if the test or prototype itself is wrong, run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>` and leave it unchanged during the night. How many rounds a criterion gets is your judgement: keep fixing the product while a fix is in sight, and when none is, give it the line `ABANDON: AC<n> failed <what each round tried>`, which **Write the closing-comment draft** puts in the draft, and carry on with the rest; the closeout counts no rounds, so that line is the whole record of the trying.
   Done when `verify-ticket.py <n>` has recorded a run of your own after the integration.
4. **Post the decisions comment.** Once: `verify-ticket.py <n> --decisions <file>`. The file is two sections, each opened by its `## ` heading: `## Decisions I made on my own`, with every line written so far, one per line, in the shape the closing comment uses; and `## Outside Owns`, with the `Outside Owns:` line of your newest run (its `ticket.checked` event, run `self`), followed by one sentence per file saying which criterion could not pass without it; `None` when that line is `None`. It is posted here, before the reviewer starts, so the review judges every line of it, and once: the review's fix round adds nothing to it, and the closing comment carries the final version.
   Done when `--decisions` exits 0.
5. **Start the reviewer.** Run the **no-comments** skill on the branch's diff and commit what it removes. Then run `dispatch.sh send reviewer <n>`, and end your turn. One reviewer per round. Exit 2 whose stderr says to start again: run it once more. Any other exit 2, or a second one: the pipeline is at fault, so open a `fault` child, and stop.
   Done when no-comments has run, `send` exited 0 and your turn has ended, or the `fault` child is open.
6. **Read the review.** When `#<n> reviewer.reported` wakes you, do the dispatch skill's `## On waking`, then read the report: `dispatch.sh wait <n> reviewer` prints the event with the two commits it read, then the report. When `#<n> reviewer.lost` wakes you instead, the reviewer died with no report: do `## On waking`, then go back to **Start the reviewer**. A report whose first lines name a failure (a commit that does not resolve, an empty diff) in place of the axes' reports is no review: open a `fault` child and stop. The in-ticket round is first: fix the in-ticket findings once, under the rules that governed the first write in **Read yourself in and write the code**, then run `dispatch.sh integrate <n>` as **Integrate and run every criterion** says, without running the criteria: **Run every criterion one final time** is the one run after the fix. An in-ticket finding you do not fix is answered `refuted:`, which says you checked and the bad outcome does not happen at the cited location. Write what disproves this specific claim; a true fact about nearby code that does not disprove the claim does not count. Then, for each out-of-ticket finding whose body the in-ticket round has not made untrue, run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue finding <file>`.
   Done when the review report is read and its wake acked, or a report naming a failure has its `fault` child, each in-ticket finding is fixed or `refuted:`, and each out-of-ticket finding still true has its `finding` child.
7. **Audit.** Read the ticket once more against the branch, the way the owner will read your closing comment. Read each CHECK's existing `tests/e2e/<product>/.e2e/<test file name>/results/…/trace.md` without another run. Each point under **What to build** holds in the product, not only in a test (**principle-prove-it-works**), and each baseline under **Read first** is followed where it applies. A point that does not hold: fix it now, before the final run. When no criterion covers that point, also run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>`, naming the point and what a criterion for it would check.
   Done when you can name, for each point and each baseline, where the branch follows it, and each point this step fixed is committed, with its `contract` child where no criterion covers it.
8. **Run every criterion one final time.** `verify-ticket.py <n> --reverify --actor worker`. It runs after the last step that writes a commit, including the review fix and the audit's, and includes criteria earlier runs already ticked. When nothing has been committed since your run in **Integrate and run every criterion**, skip it: the closeout reads that run. A run that exits 2 ran nothing, as in **Integrate and run every criterion**. If anything still fails, write `ABANDON: AC<n> failed` for each failure and close out `HANDOFF REQUIRED`; this final run gets no fix round.
   Done when the newest run on the ticket is at `HEAD`.
9. **Tell the tickets whose files you changed.** `verify-ticket.py <n> --touched`.
   Done when `--touched` exits 0.
10. **Write the closing-comment draft.** Answer the register-problem skill's three self-check questions before writing the comment. This ticket's product problems still use `verify-ticket.py --sub-issue`; register the others with the register-problem skill's `scripts/register.py add`. First cut loose what only a person can settle: a criterion that waits only on one sentence from a person gets `ABANDON: AC<n> decision <question, options, and the default if nobody answers>` **and** `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue decision <file>`; the ticket does not stop. Then `verify-ticket.py <n> --draft`, which prints the path it wrote as `DRAFT: wrote <path>`; give that run no path of your own. Fill every `<fill>`: `skipped:` as `[X], add when [Y]`; each review finding as `fixed <commit>` or `refuted: <what actually happens at path:line>`; `Decisions I made on my own` with every line written so far. Put each `ABANDON:` line under its criterion. The owner reads this comment in the morning, so write every line you fill per `references/handling-a-request.md` `## Writing the reply`.
    Done when the closing-comment draft has no `<fill>` left.
11. **Close the ticket.** Run `verify-ticket.py <n> --closeout <draft>`. Never close the ticket or swap its labels yourself: a hook blocks the command. Open no pull request: the orchestrator lands the ticket once it is closed, and running `land` from this session would stop it.
    Done when `--closeout` exits 0.
