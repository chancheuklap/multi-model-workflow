### Chart a map

**You own one map: its destination named with the owner, the map issue on the tracker, the decision tickets that can be stated now, and the answers of its research tickets; researcher sessions read for those, and no other ticket is resolved here.** A loose idea has arrived, too big for one session, and the way from here to its destination is not visible yet. Charting finds the questions on that way, not their answers: every ticket but a research one is resolved later, one per session, with Resolve a map ticket. What a map, a ticket, the fog and the out of scope are is the `wayfinder` skill; read it before step 1. Distinct from Write a spec, which starts from decisions already made, and from Resolve a map ticket, which works a map that exists.

1. **Name the destination.** Read the `grilling` and `domain-modeling` skills' `SKILL.md` and run this round with the owner as both describe, to pin down what the map is finding its way to: the spec, the decision, or the change. The destination fixes the scope, so it is settled first.
   Done when the owner has agreed to one or two lines that say what reaching the end of the map looks like.
2. **Map the frontier.** Grill again, **breadth-first**: fan out across the whole space rather than deep on one thread, surfacing the open decisions and the first steps takeable now. When this surfaces no fog (the way is already clear, and the whole journey fits one session), no map is needed: tell the owner, name Write a spec as the route when the decisions are made, and ask how they want to proceed; this playbook ends here.
   Done when you hold the open decisions, each either sharp enough to be a ticket or written down as fog, and what the owner ruled out of scope.
3. **Create the map.** Write the map body in the shape of the `wayfinder` skill's `### The map body`: **Destination** and **Notes** filled in, the effort's directory name in **Notes**, **Decisions so far** empty, the fog sketched into **Not yet specified**, what the owner ruled out in **Out of scope**. Publish the map as one issue with the labels `wayfinder:map` and `mmw:map`, as the repository's `docs/agents/issue-tracker.md` `## Wayfinding operations` says.
   Done when the map is on the tracker with both labels, and you hold its number.
4. **Create the tickets you can state now.** Create each as a child issue of the map, its body the `## Question` the `wayfinder` skill's `### Tickets` gives and its `wayfinder:<type>` label; then wire the blocking edges in a **second pass** (issues need ids before they can reference each other). Everything you cannot yet state sharply stays in **Not yet specified**.
   Done when every ticket you could state is a child of the map with its type label, and every blocking edge between them is wired.
5. **Answer the research tickets.** For each `research` ticket just created, write a brief from the `mmw-mode` skill's `references/map-research-brief.md`, with the ticket's question, the map's destination, and the note's path where the repository keeps research notes. Start them all in one `dispatch.sh brief researcher <file>...` call of the `dispatch` skill, then end your turn. On the wake `brief <batch> done`, follow the `dispatch` skill's `## On waking`. For each answer: save it at its note path, commit it on the base branch and push; post its `## Answer` and the note's path as the ticket's resolution comment and close the ticket; append its context pointer to the map's **Decisions so far**, one at a time, reading the map body fresh before each edit. Then `dispatch.sh brief close <batch>`.
   Done when every research ticket is closed with its note pushed and its pointer on the map, or the map has none.

**Reply:** the map's title and link; its destination; the tickets on the frontier now and the ones blocked behind them, by name; what is still fog and what was ruled out of scope; the research answers in one line each. The next move is a fresh session running Resolve a map ticket on this map.
