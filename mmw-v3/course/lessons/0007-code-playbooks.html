第 7 课 · 写产品代码的四份 playbook

做票、审票、小改动、修 bug，以及测试在哪一步写、在哪一步跑

v3 里写产品代码的路有四条：夜里的 worker 做一张票（Work a ticket），reviewer 审这张票（Review a ticket），你在场时不开票做一处小改动（Make a small change），你报来一个缺陷时诊断它、写成一张票、落地（Bug fix）。这一课写的是 mmw-rebuild 分支上这四份现在的样子：每一步写什么、从哪来，写代码和写测试的规矩放在哪，仓库检查什么时候跑。四份的全文都在页里，是构建时从仓库读出来的。最后三节写设计哪里错过、怎样证明的、还没做的。

这一课回答
  1. 四份 playbook 各交付什么，从哪来
  2. 一张票从认领到合进 base，worker 和 reviewer 各做哪几步，票上留下什么
  3. Work a ticket 每一步写什么
  4. 写代码的规矩各放哪：哪些成了原则，哪些留在步骤里
  5. Review a ticket 的五步和三条轴
  6. Make a small change，和夜里的一张票差在哪
  7. Bug fix，和 pstack 的差在哪
  8. 写测试的规则分在哪一步
  9. 仓库检查什么时候跑，一夜跑几次
  10. 引进了什么技能，没引进什么
  11. 设计哪里错过，怎样证明的，还没做的
依据本分支现在的 mmw-v3/skills/ 和 mmw-v3/imports.tsv；v2 在 9df1ab67d 的 implement/SKILL.md、code-review/references/session.md 和几条轴；pstack 在 e43c7ee2 的 bug-fix.md、feature.md、refactoring.md 和几条原则；mattpocock/skills 在 d81f3a18 的 tdd 和 diagnosing-bugs。这一课的每一句另请子代理对照仓库核过，查出的不符逐条改了文件或改了课文（第 11 节）。本课自己的判断标了 归纳。

2　一张票从认领到合进 base

图里要看三处。

第一，worker 第 5 步开了 reviewer 就结束回合，什么也不等。叫醒它的是 reviewer 第 5 步写下的 reviewer.reported，中继看到这个事件才把 worker 叫回来。所以 reviewer 第 2 步要拿住回合等每条轴交回：报告没贴出来，worker 永远不会醒。reviewer 死了没交报告时，看门进程写 reviewer.lost，worker 被叫醒后回到第 5 步再开一个。

第二，三条轴是三个子代理，同时开，互相看不到。v2 的 session.md 写了为什么，这一句现在在 Review a ticket 第 5 步：the separation is what keeps a passing axis from covering a failing one。一处改动可以照足了仓库的写法却做错了东西（Standards 过，Spec 不过），也可以做对了东西、测试却怎么写都会过（Standards 和 Spec 过，Tests 不过）。

第三，worker 的会话可能中途死掉，orchestrator 会开一个新的 worker 接着做。新 worker 第 1 步跑 --preflight，verify-ticket.py 读票上的事件，打出一行 RESUME:，按标题指回第 3 到 8 步之一。这六个标题写在 verify-ticket.py 的 RESUME_STEPS 里，check-interfaces.py 第 4 条每次都查 playbook 里有没有它们，所以这六步的标题不能改。第 1、2 步不用回去：没有任何运行记录时就是新认领；第 9 到 11 步重做也不坏事。

mmw-v3/skills/mmw-mode/playbooks/work-a-ticket.md 全文
### Work a ticket

**You own one ticket, as its worker, from its claim to its closing comment; its reviewer is dispatched from here.** A script started you on it and nobody is watching, so the mode's **Unattended** paragraph holds from the first step. Distinct from Review a ticket, which judges this work and writes nothing but its report.

Commands of the `verify-ticket` skill's `verify-ticket.py` and the `dispatch` skill's `dispatch.sh` are named bare below. Every `--sub-issue` run is in the `verify-ticket` skill's `references/sub-issues.md`. A fault in the pipeline itself (its own scripts, a hook, or `.mmw/target.json`): `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue fault <file>`, whose body is the command you ran and the output you saw, then stop.

1. **Claim the ticket and find your step.** Run `verify-ticket.py <n> --preflight`. On `NOT_READY`, stop: the reason is already on the ticket, as a `ticket.refused` event that wakes the orchestrator. A `RESUME:` line names, by its title, the step below to carry on at; where you are is what the ticket's events say, not what this session remembers (**principle-progress-is-what-the-record-says**). With no `RESUME:` line, go on to the next step. The branch may already carry the commits of an earlier worker of this ticket whose session ended, among them a `wip(#<n>): uncommitted work of …` commit holding what it left uncommitted. They are this ticket's work: carry on from them.
   Done when `--preflight` claimed the ticket and you are at the step its `RESUME:` line names or the next one, or it printed `NOT_READY` and you stopped.
2. **Read yourself in and write the code.** Read until you know what this ticket delivers, what it must not contradict, and the words the repository uses for them: the ticket in full, comments included, and its own open sub-issues (`gh api --paginate repos/{owner}/{repo}/issues/<n>/sub_issues?per_page=100 --jq '.[] | select(.state=="open")'`), as a supplement to **What to build** (whether you do them goes under `Decisions I made on my own`); then every item under its **Read first**, each through to its conclusion: the part of a research file that answers its question, an ADR's decision in the untitled paragraph under its title, a design package pulled into the repository, a prototype's leaf `README.md` read to its verdict. Then follow **Parent** to the spec: read its Problem Statement and Solution, the few lines that say who uses this and why, then only the Implementation Decisions sections the ticket names, plus its Testing Decisions and Out of Scope, not the whole spec. Where the ticket is silent, choose what serves the person the Problem Statement names. Then the domain glossary (the root `CONTEXT.md`, or the `CONTEXT.md` of each context `CONTEXT-MAP.md` lists that this ticket touches): where it has a term for the thing you name, use that term. Open the Memory records your start prompt lists that bear on this ticket, as `references/memory.md` says. Read the repository's `CODING_STANDARDS.md` in full, its `## Tests` section included: the review judges your code and your tests by it.
   Write the code test-first with the `tdd` skill, at the seams the ticket's **Seam** names. Where an acceptance criterion's `CHECK:` names a test case, that case is your first red test: run it before writing the code it covers and read why it fails (**principle-a-check-must-be-able-to-fail**). A criterion that is a pin is run green before the structure moves, and broken once by hand to see it red. No later step does this for you: the claim-time baseline ran before the test existed, and the review reads tests without running them. While writing:
   - Every baseline under **Read first**, and every spec section **Parent** names, is followed as **principle-baseline-is-the-contract** says; a prototype is rewritten to production standard, keeping the shape its verdict settled on. Where one does not hold, or an acceptance criterion lacks a state, field, interaction or case or contradicts another such source, keep going and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>`. When the rest of the ticket depends on the answer, end your turn: the child wakes the orchestrator, which corrects the source and resumes you, or leaves the question to the owner. A wrong `CHECK:` is a `contract` child too: name the acceptance criterion, quote what is wrong, and state what it should be. Once a batch is published, only its orchestrator or the owner edits a spec, ticket body or acceptance criterion.
   - Put no question on the screen (**principle-never-block-on-the-human**). Take the option the ticket, its baselines and the spec make most likely, write one line for it under `Decisions I made on my own`, and keep going. A question whose answer would change what the ticket delivers gets `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue decision <file>` instead, and the rest of the work carries on. Write each line for the reviewer, who judges it, and for the owner, who reads it in the morning without your context: a choice the owner would notice in the product (a label, a default, what a page shows or hides) is the one most worth a line.
   - Change a function under **principle-migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis**; write a helper under **principle-start-from-what-exists**, searching the repository and **Read first**. Before adding a file, a dependency or a configuration entry, write under `Decisions I made on my own` why the existing one is not enough (**principle-laziness-protocol**).
   - Whatever you simplify, keep intact: security, error handling that prevents data loss, accessibility, and anything the ticket explicitly asks for: **What to build**, every acceptance criterion, the baseline, the interface under **Seam**.
   - A file outside **Owns**: change it when a criterion cannot pass otherwise, and it lands under `Outside Owns:` in the closing comment; when the change is merely convenient, leave it and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue deferred <file>`. A file owned by a ticket that can run beside this one (neither blocks the other, directly or down a chain) is never changed from here, whatever needs it: run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>`, because one file has one writer at a time and the cut missed an edge (**principle-separate-before-serializing-shared-state**).
   - Run typechecking and single test files regularly, and, once at the end, the tests the repository's own instructions name for what this ticket changed (**principle-run-the-smallest-test-set**); the repository's `checks` run when `advance`, or `land` outside a night, merges the ticket into the base branch. Scratch scripts and quick checks need not be kept. Commit tests only where the ticket asks for them or this repository already keeps tests for this kind of change, sized like the neighbouring test files: roughly one focused test per stated behaviour. This is about extras only: implement every behaviour the ticket asks for, completely.
   Done when every point under **What to build** has its code and the tests the ticket asks for, committed to the ticket's branch.
3. **Integrate and run every criterion.** Integrate `origin/<base branch>` first: `dispatch.sh integrate <n>`, from this ticket's worktree. Exit 3 leaves a conflicted merge in the tree. The incoming side is closed tickets whose criteria the closing pass's `reverify` runs again on the base branch, so read each incoming ticket and its closing comment before you resolve, and keep what landed: a resolution that drops their behaviour reopens their ticket hours later, and after a bounce no reviewer sees this merge. Where theirs and yours cannot both hold, the cut missed an edge: keep what landed and run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue contract <file>` naming both tickets. Note each trade-off under `Decisions I made on my own`, run the tests that cover the files the merge conflicted in, commit the merge, and run `dispatch.sh integrate <n>` again. Exit 2 names the condition that prevented integration; a pipeline failure becomes a `fault` sub-issue, then stop. Never rebase, abort or push from this integration. Then run every criterion: `verify-ticket.py <n>`. A criterion passes only when its `CHECK:` exits 0 and its output matches `EXPECT:`. A run that exits 3 found no product slot free and ran nothing: end your turn, and when `#<n> worker.queued` wakes you, do the `dispatch` skill's `## On waking`, then run the same command again. How many rounds a criterion gets is your judgement: keep fixing the product while a fix is in sight (**principle-fix-the-product-not-the-check**), and when none is, give it the line `ABANDON: AC<n> failed <what each round tried>` (the `verify-ticket` skill's `## ABANDON lines`), which step 10 puts in the closing-comment draft, and carry on with the rest.
   Done when `verify-ticket.py <n>` has recorded a run of your own after the integration.
4. **Post the decisions comment.** Once: `verify-ticket.py <n> --decisions <file>`. The file is two sections, each opened by its `## ` heading: `## Decisions I made on my own`, with every line written so far, one per line, in the shape the closing comment uses; and `## Outside Owns`, with the `Outside Owns:` line of your newest run (its `ticket.checked` event, run `self`), followed by one sentence per file saying which criterion could not pass without it; `None` when that line is `None`. It is posted here, before the reviewer starts, so the review judges every line of it, and once: the review's fix round adds nothing to it, and the closing comment carries the final version.
   Done when `--decisions` exits 0.
5. **Start the reviewer.** Run `dispatch.sh start <n> reviewer`, then end your turn. One reviewer per round. Exit 2 whose stderr says to start again: run it once more. Any other exit 2, or a second one: the pipeline is at fault, so open a `fault` child as the `verify-ticket` skill's `references/sub-issues.md` says, and stop.
   Done when `start` exited 0 and your turn has ended, or the `fault` child is open.
6. **Read the review.** When `#<n> reviewer.reported` wakes you, do the `dispatch` skill's `## On waking`, then read the report: `dispatch.sh wait <n> reviewer` prints the event with the two commits it read. When `#<n> reviewer.lost` wakes you instead, the reviewer died with no report: do `## On waking`, then go back to **Start the reviewer**. The in-ticket round is first: fix the in-ticket findings once, under the rules that governed the first write in **Read yourself in and write the code**, and run **Integrate and run every criterion** again. An in-ticket finding you do not fix is answered `refuted:`, which says you checked and the bad outcome does not happen at the cited location. Write what disproves this specific claim; a true fact about nearby code that does not disprove the claim does not count. Then, for each out-of-ticket finding whose body the in-ticket round has not made untrue, run `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue finding <file>`.
   Done when the review report is read and its wake acked, each in-ticket finding is fixed or `refuted:`, and each out-of-ticket finding still true has its `finding` child.
7. **Run every criterion one final time.** `verify-ticket.py <n> --reverify --actor worker`. It runs after the last step that writes a commit, including the review fix, and includes criteria earlier runs already ticked. If anything still fails, write `ABANDON: AC<n> failed` for each failure and close out `HANDOFF REQUIRED`; this final run gets no fix round.
   Done when the final run is on the ticket at `HEAD`.
8. **Audit.** Read the ticket once more against the branch, the way the owner will read your closing comment. Each point under **What to build** holds in the product, not only in a test (**principle-prove-it-works**), and each baseline under **Read first** is followed where it applies. A point that does not hold is said in the closing comment; nothing is committed after the final run.
   Done when you can name, for each point and each baseline, where the branch follows it or the line of the closing comment that says it does not.
9. **Tell the tickets whose files you changed.** `verify-ticket.py <n> --touched`.
   Done when `--touched` exits 0.
10. **Write the closing-comment draft.** First cut loose what only a person can settle: a criterion that waits only on one sentence from a person gets `ABANDON: AC<n> decision <question, options, and the default if nobody answers>` **and** `verify-ticket.py <n> --sub-issue decision <file>`; the ticket does not stop. Then `verify-ticket.py <n> --draft`, which prints the path it wrote as `DRAFT: wrote <path>`; give that run no path of your own. Fill every `<fill>`: `skipped:` as `[X], add when [Y]`; each review finding as `fixed <commit>` or `refuted: <what actually happens at path:line>`; each criterion under `Green before work:` with the landed ticket that did that work, with the fact that it is a pin for behaviour this ticket keeps, or with the fact that this `CHECK:` cannot see this ticket's behaviour (then also a `contract` child); `Decisions I made on my own` with every line written so far. Put each `ABANDON:` line under its criterion.
    Done when the closing-comment draft has no `<fill>` left.
11. **Close the ticket.** Run `verify-ticket.py <n> --closeout <draft>`. It posts the closing comment and puts `ticket.passed` or `ticket.returned` on the ticket, which wakes the orchestrator to land it. Never close the ticket or swap its labels yourself: a hook blocks the command. Open no pull request, and do not run `land` from this session: it stops every session the ticket's events name, this one included.
    Done when `--closeout` exits 0.

**Reply:** the closing comment `--closeout` posted is this run's reply; this session's own last message is read by nobody.

### Review a ticket

**You own one review report on one ticket, as its reviewer; the axes are dispatched from here.** The ticket's worker started you and is asleep until your report is on the ticket, and nobody else is watching, so the mode's **Unattended** paragraph holds from the first step. Distinct from Work a ticket: you change no code and write nothing but the report.

Your start prompt names the ticket and the base commit, and lists the reviewer Rules the owner approved, or `none`. Apply each Rule only within its stated scope, to decide what to inspect, and finish only when every applicable Rule has been applied. A Rule is never a finding's source, and neither are Memory, the worker's reasoning or its self-assessment: each finding cites the current ticket, spec, repository or check.

1. **Pin the diff.** Run `git rev-parse <base-commit>`, `git diff <base-commit>...HEAD --stat` and `git log <base-commit>..HEAD --oneline`. Three dots, so the comparison runs against the merge-base. A ref that does not resolve, or an empty diff, is a failure here, before the axes spend a context each on nothing: post it as the report (**Post the report**), its first line with the refs as given, then one line naming the failure; then stop.
   Done when both commits resolve and the diff is not empty, or that failure is on the ticket.
2. **Run the axes.** Read the ticket. Run the `code-review` skill on the ticket and the base commit with the axes Standards, Spec and Tests. Hold your turn until every axis has reported: the worker is asleep on your report, and what wakes it is **Post the report**, which cannot run until the report exists.
   Done when every axis you started has reported.
3. **Verify every finding.** The axes read the code in slices, and some of what they report is wrong. The worker treats every in-ticket line as work to do before the ticket closes: a false finding you pass on costs a fix round and can break code that was right; a real one you withdraw ships. At the cited file and line, does the bad outcome the axis describes actually occur? Read beyond the changed lines (follow callers, guards upstream) until you can answer yes or no. A different finding about nearby code does not settle this one. Judge whether the problem is real, not whether the proposed fix is plausible. Code that loudly fails on a situation you never showed the program can reach is correct behavior, not a defect. Render exactly one conclusion: it holds (the bad outcome does occur at the cited location); `refuted` (you checked, and it does not happen there: write what disproves this specific claim, since a true fact about nearby code does not count); or you could not tell (the diff and surrounding code leave the question open). You report: you do not assign severity, fix a finding, or drop one because its fix looks large.
   Done when every finding has one conclusion.
4. **Sort every finding into in-ticket or out-of-ticket.** The split asks whose work the repair is: in-ticket means this ticket should not close with it unfixed. A finding is **in-ticket** when it touches one of six things: this ticket's acceptance criteria, a decision in the spec section the ticket names, a baseline under the ticket's `## Read first`, the spec's `## Out of Scope`, the spec's `## Testing Decisions`, or a file inside this ticket's `## Owns`. Everything else is **out-of-ticket**. A line the Spec axis marks `should not` under its `Decisions` heading is in-ticket: it is the worker's own decision or a file it changed outside `## Owns`, so this ticket is where it is undone. For a finding about a ticket merged into the base branch, apply the same boundary: a repair target inside this ticket's `## Owns` is in-ticket; one that lies only inside that other ticket's `## Owns` is out-of-ticket. In-ticket findings get one round of fixes on this ticket; out-of-ticket findings become `finding` children the worker opens, and block nothing.
   Done when every finding that holds or could not be told is in one of the two lists.
5. **Post the report.** Write the report to a file and run the `verify-ticket` skill's `verify-ticket.py <ticket> --review <file>`. It posts the report and puts `reviewer.reported` on the ticket, which wakes the worker; it is the only way this review reaches anyone. The report's first line is fixed: `REVIEW <base-commit>..<HEAD commit>`. Then the axis reports under `## Standards`, `## Spec` and `## Tests`, verbatim or lightly cleaned, in that order. Then `## Withdrawn`, each `refuted` finding with the refutation that disproves that specific claim. Then `## In-ticket` and `## Out-of-ticket`, every finding in this exact shape:

   ```
   - <Standards|Spec|Tests> [<category>] <path>:<line> — <claim> — source: <URL|path:line|CHECK evidence>
   ```

   The category is the name the axis gave the finding: its smell, its Tests shape, its Spec kind, or `documented-standard` for a breach of a rule the repository documents; a Standards finding that is none of these is `less-code` or `pass-through`. A finding with no code location of its own (a `Missing`, a `should not` on a line of `Decisions I made on my own`) cites the `## Owns` file its fix would land in, as `<path>:1`. The source is the current URL, `path:line`, or `CHECK` evidence that proves the finding; when you could not tell, append `unverified: <what would settle it>` to the same line. An empty list says `None`. End with one line per axis: how many findings it raised and the worst one within that axis. Rank nothing across axes and merge nothing between them: the separation is what keeps a passing axis from covering a failing one.
   Done when `--review` exits 0.

**Reply:** the report `--review` posted is this run's reply; this session's own last message is read by nobody.

### Make a small change

**You own one change the owner asked for while here, from their words to a commit on the base branch; a reader who did not write it reviews it.** No spec, no ticket and no night stand behind it: no criteria a script runs, no reviewer in a session of its own, no closed ticket as the record. What this session misses reaches the owner unless that reader finds it first. The temptation is to call the change too small for that reader; run **Get a second reading** however small it is. Distinct from Bug fix, which starts from a defect the owner reports, and from Run one ticket, which runs a written ticket.

1. **Write down the change and judge its size.** Paste the owner's request word for word into a file: it is this change's spec, and the request the review reads. The change is small when one session can finish it and it needs no product decision from the owner. When it is not, tell the owner it needs a spec (Write a spec), with what you found; this playbook ends here.
   Done when the file holds the request and the change is judged small, or the owner has heard why it is not.
2. **Write it test-first.** Read the repository's `CODING_STANDARDS.md` in full, its `## Tests` section included: the second reading judges the change by it. Run the `tdd` skill. With no ticket, write the seams under test into the request file and confirm them with the owner. A test counts as red only when it fails because the behaviour is absent (**principle-a-check-must-be-able-to-fail**). A change that must keep a behaviour as it is writes a pin before the structure moves (**principle-a-check-must-be-able-to-fail**). A change with no behaviour to test, such as wording or configuration, writes down instead the check that will prove it. Write the code under **principle-start-from-what-exists**, **principle-migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis** and **principle-laziness-protocol**, and, when the request names something already settled, **principle-baseline-is-the-contract**. Keep, for the Reply, one sentence per new file, dependency or configuration entry on why the existing one is not enough.
   Done when every behaviour the change adds or alters has a test that failed before its code was written and passes now, every behaviour it keeps has a pin you have seen go red, or the check that proves it is written down.
3. **Prove it on the product and run the affected tests.** Show, on the product, that the change does what the owner asked, in their words (**principle-prove-it-works**); while the product runs, the `ui-acceptance` skill's **Five rules while the product is running** hold. Then run the tests **principle-run-the-smallest-test-set** picks. A red test is answered by **principle-fix-the-product-not-the-check**.
   Done when the proof and the tests pass on the final code, with their output kept for the Reply.
4. **Commit it.** One commit per cause. It touches only what the cause requires and the tests that prove it. Its message quotes the line the test run printed (`ran 188 skipped 0`, not "the tests pass"). A change that exceeds those three is a ticket after all: tell the owner it needs one.
   Done when the change is committed on the base branch under those three rules.
5. **Get a second reading.** Run the `code-review` skill with the axes Standards, Spec and Tests, the commit this change started from as the base, and the request file as the request: its axes cannot read this conversation. Do not tell it which parts you believe are right. Check each finding as Review a ticket's **Verify every finding** says; fix what holds, in the commit of the cause it belongs to, and keep what does not, with its refutation, for the Reply. On a host that cannot run subagents the second reading is not done: the Reply says so and gives the command for a new session. Reading the change again in this session is not a second reading.
   Done when every axis has reported and each finding is fixed or refuted, or the Reply lists the second reading as not done.
6. **Push to the base branch.** Before pushing, run the repository's checks: each command `.mmw/target.json` lists under `checks`, from the repository root, with `MMW_BASE_REF=origin/<base branch>`. A red check is answered by **principle-fix-the-product-not-the-check**. When the push is rejected, fetch, merge the new base into your commit, run the affected tests and the checks again, and push again.
   Done when the commit is on the base branch on origin, and the checks passed on that commit or the repository declares none.

**Reply:** what changed, and why each new file or dependency was needed; the failing-then-passing test output and the proof from step 3, verbatim; each review finding and what became of it; what the owner should check by hand; and, when it was not done, that the second reading is outstanding.

### Bug fix

**You own the diagnosis, the ticket that carries it, and the proof on the product; the fix is a worker's, landed through Run one ticket.** Be scientific: every line of the ticket traces to runtime evidence, and a change that "might help" is a hypothesis, not a fix. Distinct from Make a small change, which changes behaviour the owner wants different, and from Investigation, which answers and changes nothing.

1. **Reproduce it yourself.** Follow the `diagnosing-bugs` skill's Phase 1 and Phase 2 on the surface where the owner saw the bug; while the product runs, the `ui-acceptance` skill's **Five rules while the product is running** hold. Drive it yourself even when a debugging protocol says to ask the owner to reproduce; ask the owner only with a stated reason the surface cannot reach the bug, and only after driving it as far as it goes. When no loop can be built, tell the owner what you tried and what you need, as Phase 1 says; this playbook ends here.
   Done when one command, already run, shows the owner's exact symptom on a minimised scenario, or the owner has heard what you tried and what you need.
2. **Find the cause.** Follow `diagnosing-bugs` Phase 3 and Phase 4. Seed the hypotheses with the `how` skill over the affected subsystem and the `why` skill for the change that brought the bug in. Show the ranked list to the owner and carry on without waiting. Confirm the surviving mechanism with runtime evidence, then remove every tagged debug line. When the code does what was asked and the owner wants it otherwise, it is not a bug: tell the owner it is a change, for Make a small change or Write a spec; this playbook ends here.
   Done when one hypothesis survives on runtime evidence, the others are ruled out, and no debug line is left.
3. **Write the ticket.** Write one ticket in the format of the `verify-ticket` skill's `references/ticket-format.md`, with `None` under **Parent**. **What to build** states the symptom, the cause with its evidence, and the smallest change that evidence justifies. **Seam** is where a regression test exercises the bug as it happens at the call site (`diagnosing-bugs` Phase 5); when there is no such seam, say so there, since that is itself a finding. The first criterion is the regression test at that seam; when the step 1 command runs unattended and gives the same verdict every run, it is the second. Publish it with the labels `mmw:ticket` and `ready-for-agent`, and the worker label the format's rule gives; then run `verify-ticket.py <n> --lint` and fix every `ERROR`.
   Done when the ticket is on the tracker and `--lint` reports no `ERROR`.
4. **Land it.** Run **Run one ticket** on this ticket.
   Done when its `land` has exited 0.
5. **Prove it on the same surface.** On the base branch, with the fix landed, run the step 1 command on the original scenario, not the minimised one. "Inconclusive", or a check on another surface, is not a pass (**principle-prove-it-works**): say so.
   Done when the original repro runs green where it ran red, or the Reply says why that could not be shown.

**Reply:** what was broken, the root cause, the fix (the ticket and the commit `land` made), and how you verified it; paste the failing and the passing repro output verbatim.

8　写测试的规则分在哪一步

和写测试有关的规则分散在这些东西里：tdd 的三份文件、四条原则、票格式里写判据的一节、verify-ticket.py、Tests 轴，以及仓库自己的 CODING_STANDARDS.md 的 ## Tests 一节和 .mmw/target.json 的 checks。分法只有一条：一条规则放在需要它的那一刻，由那一刻的步骤点名；几刻都要的规则只放一份文件，每一刻点名同一份，不各写一遍。

这条分法落到三样东西上：

  • 仓库的测试规矩只有一份文件。CODING_STANDARDS.md 的 ## Tests 一节，在三刻被点名：写判据（ticket-format.md）、写测试（两份 playbook 的第 2 步）、审测试（Tests 轴）。写的人和审的人读同一份，worker 不会从审查意见里第一次知道一条规矩。
  • 能不能失败只写在一条原则里。principle-a-check-must-be-able-to-fail 被写判据、写测试、审测试三刻点名；pin 写在它里面，这三刻就都拿到了。Tests 轴审每个用例先问它的那个问题，这是从 pstack 的 principle-test-behavior-not-implementation 来的检验。
  • 仓库检查不当判据。ticket-format.md 写明，仓库自己的整树检查（一个 lint.sh、一次全量类型检查）不能当 CHECK:：它会在这张票没碰的文件上变红，让这张票卡在别人的工作上。它在合并时由脚本跑（下一节）。
  • 仓库检查只在树要落地时跑。见下一节。

9　仓库检查什么时候跑

worker 关票之前那几次判据的运行，各在不同的代码上回答不同的问题：认领时的基线证明判据能红；第 3 步的运行给审查一份证据；第 7 步证明审查修完后的最终提交。这一节说的是另一样东西：仓库自己的检查（.mmw/target.json 的 checks，一般是 lint、类型检查、整套测试），它不看哪张票，看整棵树。规则只有一条：每棵要落进 base 或项目分支的代码树，仓库检查跑一次；树没变，就不重跑。ADR 0023 写明，仓库检查必须看合并结果，因为合得干净不等于合起来还对。

  • 关票时不跑。那棵树多半不会原样落地：夜里别的票还在落地。代价是一处离这张票很远的测试被它弄红，要到合并时才发现，票被退回，再由一个新 worker 做一轮。worker 第 2 步已经跑了改动相关的测试和类型检查，离得远的测试被弄红是少数情况。归纳
  • 合并进 base 时每张票跑一次，跑在合并结果上：夜里由 advance，夜外由 land。红了退回这张票，base 不动。
  • 你自己推到 base 的树，推之前跑一次，被拒、合并了新的 base 后再跑：Make a small change 第 6 步，Run a night 收尾自己修的、reverify 红票的修复（第 8 课）。
  • 一夜收尾的 reverify 重跑的是每张票自己的判据，不是仓库检查。判据里有仓库检查覆盖不到的（产品上跑的、查 tracker 的）；仓库没声明 checks 时它是唯一的一道。
  • finish 只在合出来的树没检查过时跑。判断用两个条件：项目分支已经在 base 里；base 的顶端正是某张票落地时 dispatch.sh 合出来的那个合并提交（读那张票 ticket.landed 里记的 merge）。两条都成立就不跑，否则跑。只看第一条不够：收尾时你自己修的东西推在最后一张票落地之后，那棵树脚本没检查过。

一夜 N 张票、收尾时没有自己修东西：仓库检查跑 N 次。v2 是大约 2N+1 次：关票时每张一次，合并时 base 动过就再跑一次（夜里几乎每张都动过），finish 再一次。收尾时自己修了一处并推到 base，多两次：推之前一次，finish 一次，这两次检查的是同一棵树。要省掉 finish 那一次，得让手动推送也在 tracker 上留一条检查过的记录；一夜里这种推送一般只有几次，我判断不值得为它加一种事件。归纳 这些次数假定这一夜没有票被退回重做；退回一次，那张票合并时多跑一次。

14　自测

1．worker 跑判据，AC2 一直红。查下来，是 AC2 的 CHECK: 本身写错了一个文件名。worker 该怎么做？

写错的判据也是一种 baseline 不成立。worker 不改票上的判据（principle-fix-the-product-not-the-check），也不能硬凑；它开 contract 子票，点名这条判据，引用写错的地方，写出它应该是什么。

2．Spec 轴报「AC3 的空列表状态没实现」。reviewer 到代码里看，列表为空时页面显示的正是 AC3 要的那句话。reviewer 该怎么做？

第 3 步：坏结果不会发生，结论是 refuted，写出推翻它的事实，放进 ## Withdrawn。原样转过去会让 worker 白修一轮；直接删掉，worker 和你都看不到它被核过。

3．你报来「导出的文件少了最后一行」。Bug fix 第 2 步查下来，代码照 spec 只导出已确认的行，最后一行是草稿。接下来怎么做？

代码照要求在做，你想要的是不同的行为，那是一处改动。Bug fix 在第 2 步停下告诉你；要你做产品决定（草稿该不该导出）就走 spec。

4．一张票只把一个模块挪了位置，行为不该变。它的判据在认领时就是绿的。worker 第 10 步怎样解释，第 2 步又该做过什么？

这条判据是 pin：钉住要保住的行为，动工前后都该绿。它能失败的证据，是 worker 在第 2 步手动把那个行为弄坏一次、看到它红。类型检查和 lint 不算 pin。

5．一夜 5 张票，项目分支没动过，收尾时你自己修了一处、推到 base。从关票到 finish，仓库检查一共跑几次？

5 张票合并时各一次；你推之前一次；finish 时 base 的顶端是你那个提交，不是某张票的合并提交，所以再跑一次。共 7 次。
