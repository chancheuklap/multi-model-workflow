# Everything at Hand

On any page or diagram for a person, put everything needed to understand a place where the reader's eyes stop at that place. Count each "remember here, look elsewhere, come back" as a defect.

**Why:** Working memory is small, and the reader forgets the first place while looking elsewhere.

**Pattern:**

- Put explanations beside what they explain. Label marks directly and keep any legend with the diagram it explains. Explain an unfamiliar term once beside its use in each diagram. Put fields, questions, and tradeoffs beside their screen or step, so the reader can understand that part in one view.
- Mark questions, changes, and decisions at their exact positions in the diagram. Give a numbered mark a short label beside it.
- Let pictures carry the content. Draw flows, structures, routes, who reads or writes what, and comparisons. Use tables for unrelated parallel entries. Use concrete box labels. Words supply what pictures cannot: the question answered, the part to inspect, and the conclusion. Remove sentences that repeat the picture. Cite source detail rather than retelling it. This keeps paragraphs from replacing the thing the reader needs to see.
- Keep one subject in view at a time. In a reference diagram, draw the components a reader needs to follow the route. Use shape or color to distinguish kinds, and label omissions that would change the reader's interpretation. Put the detail beside the part it explains. Compare before and after at the same scale, side by side when they fit.
- For a page documenting the current design, use [principle-files-describe-the-present](../principle-files-describe-the-present/SKILL.md).
- Lead with the question the picture answers or the decision the reader needs to make.

Use [principle-anchor-every-reference](../principle-anchor-every-reference/SKILL.md) for names.

**The test:** Starting at any mark on the page, can the reader say what it is and why it is there without scrolling, turning a page, or remembering the chat?

**Stop:** Use prose when it explains the fact directly. A drawing earns its space by explaining more than the prose would.

Distinct from [principle-write-for-where-it-is-read](../principle-write-for-where-it-is-read/SKILL.md), which decides who reads and what they can see. This principle governs whether everything needed while reading a page is in view.
